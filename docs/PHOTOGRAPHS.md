# Photographs in the narrative, release 2.3.0

Three local image assets provide visual pauses at different points in the exhibition:

1. **After the research introduction:** Mogao exterior, photographed in 2006 by Yaohua2000. The caption identifies the Cave 96 pavilion and keeps this site view distinct from Cave 17.
2. **Before the relationship guide:** Charles Nouette’s 1908 photograph of Pelliot examining manuscripts inside Cave 17. The portrait is displayed within its original resolution and aspect ratio.
3. **Before preservation records:** a historic composite of Cave 16 and manuscript bundles, made from two 1907 negatives by Aurel Stein. The caption identifies the composite and the location; it is not used to infer the condition of a specific manuscript today.

The full author, source, date, rights statement, byte size and SHA-256 are in [asset provenance](asset-provenance.json). The contemporary exterior image is used under CC BY-SA 3.0; its attribution and licence are linked beside the image. The two historical photographs are identified as public domain in their Commons records. Image bytes are unchanged; no crop, recolouring, reconstruction or synthetic imagery is applied. The Cave 16 identification and composite context were also checked against [Foong Ping, Figure 1](https://silkroadsarchaeoheritage.org/articles/10.5334/srah.7#F1).

All three images are embedded in both offline exports. The website uses lazy loading, explicit dimensions and local files, with no runtime requests to Wikimedia or museum image servers. The viewer uses the same local file and provides bilingual alt text, caption, credits, a close button, Escape-to-close and focus return to the opening button. On small screens, photographs and narrative text form a single reading column.

The gallery supplements the narrative. It does not add edges or evidence IDs to the historical Gephi snapshot.

# Reading and research use, release 2.7.0

The exhibition should help a visitor understand manuscript circulation and scholarly work, then continue into the sources. Maps and the Gephi network remain central, directly visible reading aids. Software names, counts and file downloads do not provide the main narrative headings.

## Reading order

1. The opening question concerns what happened after manuscripts left the Library Cave. The Mogao photograph introduces the place, with the Cave 96 exterior kept distinct from Cave 17.
2. The route chapter asks where the manuscripts were sent. Its default is the interactive route view, using the project’s QGIS-related geographical records. Destinations are stated in route choices, while transport arrival and departmental accession remain distinct. The original QGIS export and PDF remain under the regional-reference tab.
3. The 1909 Beijing–Lanzhou telegram is directly readable beside the route discussion, with its source and unresolved receipt/execution dates. A contextual action locates the same event on the map; reading the claim does not require that action.
4. Nouette’s photograph introduces examination and recording before the network chapter. The complete 122-node, 136-relationship Gephi graph remains outside disclosures. Its chapter asks who examined and organised the materials, with a visible explanation of event-mediated roles.
5. Pelliot’s memorandum and the London cataloguing team follow the graph as short, directly readable passages. Named roles, source pages and limits accompany each. A single contextual action locates `KE007` in the full graph, keeping all nodes and edges available.
6. Collecting institutions, manuscript preservation and the historic bundle image continue the story into present-day collections. Official collection links remain available for follow-up.
7. A further-reading section links three historical questions to existing source records and specific pages: Stein, volume II, pp. 916–917; Helen Wang, pp. 215–216; Qin Hualin, p. 48. The full source list and data downloads remain available.

The visitor can read the main claims without selecting a node or opening a card. Interactions have a stated purpose: compare a route, locate the record being discussed, inspect a source, or continue to a collection catalogue. Playback and advanced filters remain secondary.
